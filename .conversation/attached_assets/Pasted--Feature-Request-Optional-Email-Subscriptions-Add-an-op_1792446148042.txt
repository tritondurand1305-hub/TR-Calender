# Feature Request: Optional Email Subscriptions

Add an optional email subscription feature to the Tumbler Ridge Community Calendar.

## 1. Core requirements

- Visitors can browse the calendar, search events, view event details, and submit community announcements without creating an account.
- Email subscriptions are entirely optional and must not block access to any calendar feature.
- Provide a clearly labelled **“Get Community Updates by Email”** section.
- Make the signup form mobile-friendly, lightweight, and easy for less tech-savvy residents to use.

## 2. Signup preferences

Include these preferences, with the following defaults:

- **Weekly community digest:** Enabled by default.
- **Sports and recreation:** Disabled by default.
- **Family and youth:** Disabled by default.

Users must be able to change their selections before submitting. Explain briefly what each category includes.

Do not interpret a preselected checkbox as consent to receive email. The user must actively submit the form, and the subscription must be confirmed by email.

## 3. Consent and confirmation

Implement a proper double opt-in process:

1. The visitor enters their email address and selects their preferred updates.
2. Show a clear consent statement explaining what emails they will receive and how they can unsubscribe.
3. Send a confirmation email to the supplied address.
4. Keep the subscription inactive until the recipient clicks the confirmation link.
5. Use a cryptographically secure, single-use confirmation token with an expiration time. Store a hash of the token rather than the raw token.
6. Show a confirmation page after successful activation.
7. Do not send newsletters or digests to unconfirmed addresses.

Avoid revealing whether an email address is already subscribed. Add reasonable rate limits and protections against automated submissions.

## 4. Secure storage and privacy

Store subscriber records in the application's persistent database, not in browser storage or a temporary in-memory list.

Store only the information needed to operate the subscription service, such as:
- Email address.
- Selected categories and digest preference.
- Confirmation status and relevant timestamps.
- Consent and subscription status.
- Unsubscribe status and token hashes as required.

Apply appropriate access controls. Never expose subscriber email addresses through public API endpoints, calendar pages, client-side code, logs, or the admin interface to unauthorized users.

Keep email delivery credentials and signing secrets in server-side environment variables. Never commit secrets to GitHub.

Do not collect a subscriber's name, phone number, or other personal information unless a later feature genuinely requires it.

## 5. Unsubscribe and preference management

- Include a clearly visible unsubscribe link in every subscription email.
- Provide a secure, straightforward way to unsubscribe without creating an account or signing in.
- Support changing category preferences and the weekly digest setting.
- Make unsubscribe links secure and difficult to guess. Avoid putting email addresses or other personal information in URLs.
- Make unsubscribe actions effective promptly and ensure unsubscribed users are excluded from all future campaigns.
- Prevent unsubscribe tokens from being reused to modify another person's subscription.
- Provide a clear confirmation after a successful unsubscribe.

## 6. Canadian anti-spam compliance

Design the feature to support compliance with Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL).

- Obtain express consent before sending recurring commercial electronic messages, and retain evidence of when and how consent was obtained.
- Clearly identify the sender and provide valid contact information in subscription emails.
- Include a functioning unsubscribe mechanism in every applicable message.
- Process unsubscribe requests within the legally required timeframe and stop sending messages within that period; aim to suppress future sends immediately.
- Do not add people to the list based on their participation in a Facebook group, submission of an event, or use of the calendar.
- Keep subscription consent separate from consent to publish an event or respond to a submission.
- Review the final implementation against current CASL requirements before launch, including whether particular community messages qualify as commercial electronic messages.

## 7. Email delivery and weekly digest

Build a replaceable server-side email delivery interface so the application can use an affordable provider later without rewriting the subscription system.

For the first version:
- Implement the signup, confirmation, preferences, and unsubscribe workflows.
- Add a weekly digest generator that selects upcoming, published events within the chosen categories.
- Do not send emails automatically until a real email provider is configured and tested.
- If delivery is not configured, display a clear setup status to administrators rather than pretending emails have been sent.
- Prevent duplicate sends and record enough delivery status to diagnose failures without logging message secrets or unnecessary personal data.
- Make the sending schedule configurable, with the timezone set to `America/Vancouver`.

## 8. Integration with the existing calendar

Use the existing event database and category system where possible. Do not create a separate event database for email.

Only include published, upcoming events. Exclude drafts, rejected submissions, and expired events. Link each digest item to its public event page.

Keep the feature modular so future updates can add event-specific alerts, including short-notice sports cancellations, without requiring a rewrite of the weekly digest system. Event-specific alerts must respect category preferences and consent.

## 9. Testing and acceptance criteria

Verify that:
- Browsing and submitting events still work without an account.
- The three signup preferences use the specified defaults.
- Unconfirmed addresses receive no digest.
- Confirmation tokens expire and cannot be reused.
- Unsubscribe links work without login and prevent further sends.
- Category preferences correctly filter digest content.
- Subscriber information is protected from unauthorized access.
- Email-provider failures do not mark messages as successfully delivered.
- No real emails are sent during development unless explicitly configured for testing.
- Database migrations, environment variables, setup instructions, and tests are included in the repository README.

Inspect the existing project first and adapt to its actual framework, database, and deployment setup. Keep changes focused; do not rebuild unrelated calendar features. Document any provider, hosting, or compliance decisions that still require configuration before launch.
